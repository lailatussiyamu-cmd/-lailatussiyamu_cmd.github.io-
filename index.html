Case study · Clinical tool · PWA

PediDrip

Weight-based continuous infusion calculator for the PICU at RSUD Dr. Saiful Anwar. It computes syringe-pump rates and prints titration cards nurses tape to the pump.

Live app ↗ Code ↗

Problem

Nurses converted weight-based doses to mL/h by hand, across drugs that use different units: mcg/kg/min for epinephrine, unit/kg/h for vasopressin, mg/kg/h for furosemide. Every manual conversion at the bedside is a chance for a tenfold error.

Key decisions

  • One canonical web build as the source of truth. The Expo app is kept in sync with it, never the other way round.
  • A versioned dose table, printed in the footer and on every label, so any card can be traced to the protocol it came from.
  • A "rate too slow" warning at 0.1 mL/h, the lowest rate the unit's syringe pumps hold reliably.
  • Offline-first PWA, because ward signal is unreliable and the tool must work at 3 a.m.

Challenges

  • Features broke on phones in an early deploy. I consolidated three hosted copies into one URL so staff could not open an outdated version.
  • Aminophylline presets had to match pharmacy practice, so concentrations are built from whole 240 mg ampoules: 4.8, 12 and 24 mg/mL.
  • Reviewing every file before commit caught a bug in the "New patient" reset.

Result

Live at v2026.09.06c, installable on Android and usable without signal. Printed cards are a uniform 60 × 90 mm, nine per A4, with ISO 26825 syringe label colours and the patient's name and record number on each card. Tested on ward phones and entered in the Emergent × Building Indonesia builder contest.
